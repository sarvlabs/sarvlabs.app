The name · Persian

sarv سرو

The cypress, Persia’s tree of the free.

Free

Green in every season

In the Gulistan, written in 1258, the poet Saadi explains why only the cypress is called āzād, “free”.

“They call none azad, or free, excepting the cypress, which bears no fruit.”Saadi, Gulistan, as quoted by Thoreau in Walden

It is never bound to a season of bloom and a season of withering. It simply stays green.

Eternal

Four thousand years and counting

The cypress was sacred in ancient Persia. The Cypress of Abarkuh in central Iran has stood for more than four thousand years, and may be the oldest living thing in Asia.

Upright

Tall, rooted, unbowed

Persian poets reach for the cypress when they describe a graceful, upright bearing: sarv-e ravān, the flowing cypress.

Resilient

It bends, and does not break

The paisley pattern is thought to be a cypress with its tip bent by the wind.

For us: work that stays evergreen, steady in every season.

The name · Sanskrit

sarva सर्व

All, whole, and at its root, well.

Whole

Nothing left out

Sarva means all, every, entire. It is a word of completeness.

Well

To be whole is to be well

It grows from the same ancient root as Latin salvus, “safe, in good health”, and Greek holos, “whole”. English still carries it in safe, solid and holistic.

For all

A wish for everyone

Sarve bhavantu sukhinaḥ, sarve santu nirāmayāḥ.May all be happy; may all be free from illness.

The rise of all

Progress that lifts everyone

Sarvodaya, “the rise of all”, was Gandhi’s word for progress that leaves no one behind.

For us: software for the whole person, made for everyone.
